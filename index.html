What The LLC resonant tank

An LLC converter regulates output voltage by shifting the switching frequency of a half- or full-bridge around the series resonance of three coupled reactive elements: series inductor Lr, series capacitor Cr, and magnetizing inductance Lm.

The hardest part of LLC design is not the equations — it is that m, Q, n, fr, and the ZVS dead-time requirement are all coupled. Tightening one always loosens another. The calculator shows that coupling live as you move the sliders.

New to this? Read this guide top to bottom once: the picture below shows the physical circuit, then the two cards after it explain the only two knobs you actually turn (m and Q) and the failure mode to avoid (losing ZVS). The equations on the right are reference material — you do not need to memorize them to use the calculator.

Topology The two power stages

Both selectable in the calculator. The tank and rectifier are identical; only the inverter differs, and that difference is captured entirely by Kb — the factor relating the bus the tank actually sees to Vin.

Reading the picture, left to right: the inverter (the switches on the left) chops the DC input into a square wave. That square wave drives the resonant tank — Lr, Cr and Lm — which is the actual "LLC" part and the thing this whole tool sizes. The tank's output feeds the rectifier on the right, which turns it back into DC at your output voltage. Everything the calculator computes (Lr, Cr, Lm, n) describes the middle block; the two end blocks only decide Kb.

The two design levers m and Q

Every slider in the tool affects the result, but only m and Q are genuine design degrees of freedom. Everything else is a specification.

m=(Lr+Lm) / Lrinductance ratio — the main design knob
Q=Zr / Racquality factor — set by the load, not by you

Low m = tall gain peak, easy ZVS, wide frequency range, high circulating current. High m = efficient, narrow frequency range, gain-starved. Practical designs land at m ≈ 4–8. Start at 6 and iterate.

Low Q = taller gain peak, lower-impedance tank, more boost headroom. High Q = peak drops, frequency range widens, gain margin tightens. Q is set by your load via Rac = (8/π²)·n²·Vo²/Po — it is not a free knob, it follows from the spec.

fr does not affect gain. It cancels out of the gain expression exactly — changing it scales Lr, Cr and Lm together but leaves every gain value and frequency ratio unchanged. Choose fr for magnetics size and switching loss, after m and Q are settled.

Real curves from the tool's own gain equation, m fixed at 6. Notice: every curve crosses gain=1 at Fn=1 (the dashed line) regardless of Q — that's the load-independent efficiency point discussed above. Lower Q reaches a taller peak but the peak also sits closer to resonance.

ZVS The region that matters

Above the peak-gain frequency the tank is inductive — current lags voltage, the body diode conducts before the switch turns on, and commutation is lossless (ZVS). Below the peak the tank turns capacitive: the switch turns on hard, reverse recovery spikes the current, and the gain slope inverts making control unstable. Infineon warns this "might cause high current spikes and device failure."

Inductive region is necessary but not sufficient for ZVS. During dead time, the magnetizing current must fully discharge the switch-node capacitance before the opposite device fires. This is a separate energy constraint — ½LmILm² ≥ ½CossVin² — that the gain plot cannot see. The ZVS sweep tab shows it.

The calculator What it does

Size the tank|H|

You set the turns ratio n directly and it sizes Lr, Cr and Lm from Q, m and fr. Plain FHA — nothing hidden, ideal rectifier, lossless tank.

Check itgain + ZVS

Two independent tests: peak gain must clear the boost required at Vin,min, and the magnetizing current must discharge Coss inside the dead time. The verdict panel and the feasibility map apply exactly these.

Verify ittime domain

A trapezoidal transient solve of the tank you just sized, with an explicit three-state rectifier. It reports the true peak and RMS currents that the sinusoidal approximation only estimates.

One gain expression throughout. Every tab reads the same |H| and the same single ZVS rule, so the tiles, the verdict, the map and the sim cannot disagree with each other.

Gain The FHA expression

|H| = Fn² (m−1)√ [ (mFn²−1)² + Fn²(Fn²−1)²(m−1)²Q² ]
Fn = fsw/fr· m = (Lr+Lm)/Lr· Q = Zr/Rac· Rac = (8/π²)·n²·Vo²/Po

Four things this expression makes visible:

(1) every Q curve crosses M=1 at Fn=1 — load-independent, the efficiency anchor.

(2) each curve has a peak that is the boost ceiling.

(3) lower Q reaches a higher peak — light load has more boost headroom than full load.

(4) lower m raises the peak but increases circulating current.

Why Fn=1 is special. Substituting Fn=1 into |H|: numerator = (m−1), denominator = √[(m−1)²] = (m−1), so |H|=1 exactly — for every Q and m, with no approximation. This load-independent unity-gain crossing is why the turns ratio is anchored to nominal input: the converter naturally sits at its efficiency optimum. The tool verifies this numerically against a complex-impedance solve; residual ~1e−15 shown in the Table tab.

Sizing chain The calculator

n=Kb·Vin / Voeditable — override it freely
Rac=(8/π²)·n²·Vo² / Poideal rectifier, Vf omitted (Infineon/TI convention)
Zr=Q · Rac
Lr=Zr / ωr
Cr=1 / (ωr · Zr)
Lm=(m−1) · Lr
Mreq=n·Vo / (Kb·Vin,min)must be ≤ peak gain

Kb=0.5 half-bridge, 1 full-bridge — see the topology figures near the top of this guide. The gain at fr is 1 for every Q and m, so fr is where the converter naturally sits.
